# [Direction Finding]Bi-Baseline ISAC AoA and AoD Joint Estimation Based on Complex Neural Networks [Attached Code]

Introduction: Understanding the underlying physical processes behind nonlinear phenomena has always been a key issue in nonlinear dynamics research. It is often necessary to solve corresponding inverse problems to gain new insights into certain nonlinear effects. In this sense, signal processing plays an important role, as it can be used for system identification, a well-known inverse problem. There exists a duality between the forward and backward problems: The classical Poincaré perturbation method uses form power series to quantify small deviations from linear systems to nonlinear ones, which are related to harmonic distortion in time waves, one of the most clear nonlinear indicators [2]; Fourier analysis can also reveal higher harmonics and/or subharmonics, another well-known nonlinear indicator that has already been used in many practical engineering problems [3]. The basic idea of this tool is consistent with that of harmonic balance (HB), a frequency domain nonlinear system steady-state calculation method [4]. The above two types of duality capture the fundamental characteristics of nonlinear responses [1].
In conjunction with time-domain and frequency-domain descriptions, the Time-Frequency Analysis (TFA) technique is employed to observe responses in the joint domain [5]. In this case, some common TFA methods (such as Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) and Wavelet Transform (WT)) can be utilized to track some time-varying features, such as the skeleton curve [6], instantaneous mode parameters [7], and resonance transition [8]. It is noteworthy that the aforementioned nonlinear features are slow dynamics, which give rise to systems with slow-changing amplitudes and phases [10]. This can also be obtained analytically through average methods, Krylov-Bogoliubov-Mitropolsky (KBM) methods, or multiscale analysis [4]. Contrarily, Huang et al. (1996) [11] observed a class of rapid dynamics known as wave-in-wave modulation when analyzing classical nonlinear systems (including Duffing, Lorenz, and R ssler systems) using the Hilbert-Huang Transform (HHT) method. They first observed rapid oscillations in the instantaneous frequency (IF) and/or amplitude (IA) within the time-frequency-energy domain. As Huang pointed out [11], wave-in-wave modulation captures the inherent nonlinearity associated with harmonic distortion, rather than explaining the dispersion propagation externally displayed in the wave series, which should be considered as inter-wave modulation. Wave-in-wave modulation should have physical significance and provide more detailed information than modern topological tools. However, these features are lost in Fourier analysis and vaguely described in wavelet analysis [11].
Although there is no strict analysis that directly leads to this rapid dynamic behavior, it has been proven that wave-induced modulation originates from specific approximations of the classical perturbations [12], and Bessel functions describe the quantitative relationship between the modulation characteristics and harmonic distortion [1]. Following Huang's research, researchers have observed similar phenomena in many practical nonlinear systems, such as rotor systems with friction [13] or crack faults [14], non-stationary power systems [15], marine systems [16], and even human biological systems [17, 18]: internal wave modulation arises along the nonlinearity, becoming a new tool for nonlinear detection and identification.
However, the empirical nature of HHT methods prohibits new insights into this nonlinear phenomenon. HHT employs a comprehensive framework that combines adaptive signal decomposition [19] and high-resolution time-frequency representations, utilizing EMD and Hilbert transform respectively. The special elements contained within EMD make it difficult to perform mathematical analysis. Therefore, the so-called intrinsic mode functions (IMF) extracted are often physically meaningless, especially when subject to noise perturbations. Despite this, the framework has led to numerous HHT-class methods with a solid analytical foundation, among which the Synchronous Squeezing Transform (SST) proposed by Daubichies et al. [20] is a notable attempt. SST can be seen as a reassigned STFT/WT, sharpening the approximation harmonic patterns in the spectral/scaled plots, but this approach fails to handle waveforms with rapid oscillations, rather than slow-varying amplitudes and frequencies. The latter are precisely the prerequisites for SST tools [21]. Several other works have been completed to obtain modes without performing TFA, such as the Empirical Wavelet Transform (EWT) [22] and Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) [23]. Both are limited to narrowband mode decompositions, thus not suitable for waveforms with broadband spectrum. Recently, a VNCMD method based on VMD, called Variational Nonlinear Linear Frequency Modulation Mode Decomposition (VNCMD) [24], was specifically designed for broadband multicomponent signal analysis. VNCMD follows the basic idea of VMD but uses data-driven demodulation operators before estimating its bandwidth to demodulate each mode, allowing for high-precision extraction of modulation features [13]. However, the initialization of VNCMD requires given mode numbers and initial IF guesses for each mode, limiting its practical application.
This paper combines the ideas of HHT and VNCMD to eliminate the shortcomings of both original methods. This construction leads to a descendant of VNCMD, called iterative nonlinear chirp mode decomposition (INCMD). INCMD uses a modified VNCMD optimizer to extract signal modes in a recursive manner, just as EMD [11] does, and employs the Ljung-Box Q test results [25] as the decomposition termination criterion. Based on the spectral characteristics of waveform modulated signals [13], we obtain the peak frequency in the power spectrum density (PSD) of the current signal as a constant initial intermediate frequency, thus eliminating the need for additional intermediate frequency guesswork. Case studies show that this method significantly outperforms similar technologies. Using INCMD, high-precision and strong noise robustness in capturing waveform modulation can be achieved. The extracted modulation features from INCMD not only identify real nonlinear single-degree-of-freedom and multi-degree-of-freedom systems but also simulate nonlinear single-degree-of-freedom and multi-degree-of-freedom systems. Most importantly, INCMD has a solid analytical foundation.

Conclusion: This paper focuses on introducing a high-precision, noise robust, and mathematically solid signal processing method, called INCMD, for capturing waveform modulation in nonlinear responses. In the framework of INCMD, the joint optimization scheme used in HHT is replaced by a recursive process adopted in VNCMD, making the new method more adaptable without losing its strict mathematical foundation. Moreover, we obtain prior information about the intermediate frequency of the signal through power spectral estimation and control the decomposition termination through the results of the Ljung-Box Q test. Both strategies are very effective and almost do not increase computational costs. Through a challenging numerical example, it has been proven that our method outperforms the national leading state-of-the-art tools such as SST, VMD, and ICCD. Based on the extracted waveform modulation features, INCMD helps accurately identify nonlinear S-DOF and M-DOF systems, including simulated and real ones. Some noteworthy conclusions are summarized as follows:
(1) In the S-DOF nonlinear system, INCMD can separate inter-wave and intra-wave modulations that are identifiable from disturbance analysis even in noisy environments.
(2) INCMD decomposes the response of a multi-degree-of-freedom system into physically interpretable nonlinear modes [44], where dominant modes exhibit clear intra-wave modulation, while minor modes (induced by coupling effects) are almost nonexistent. Modal-space transformation [43] can be used to recover the initial spatial subsystem from the obtained modes.
(3) With INCMD, the responses of healthy beams and cracked beams can be distinguished because the former is a standard harmonic process, while the latter shows intra-wave modulation. An effective control equation can be determined using the results of INCMD analysis. INCMD also captures hidden intra-wave modulation patterns in sinusoidal signals severely contaminated by noise, which is very interesting.
